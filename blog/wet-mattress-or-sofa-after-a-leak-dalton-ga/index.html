Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: What to Do in Dalton, GA

By the Dalton Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Furniture soaks up water and holds it. Whether it can be saved depends on the water, the time and the material.

  • 📍Dalton and nearby towns

A wet mattress or sofa after a leak is a common problem, and the answer to saving it depends on three things. What kind of water it was, how long it sat and what the piece is made of. A cushion that got clean water for an hour is a different case from a sofa that sat in flood water overnight.

This guide helps you decide what can be dried, what should go and how to document it. It is general guidance, and a restoration crew can inspect pieces in person.

Start with the water category

Water damage is grouped by how clean the water is. Clean water from a supply line is the best case. Gray water from a washer drain or a dishwasher is more contaminated, and flood or sewage water is the worst. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard uses three categories.

The category decides the answer for soft furnishings. Porous items soaked in contaminated water are usually discarded because they cannot be cleaned inside. The same item wet with clean water may be saved if it is dried fast.

Mattresses

A mattress has foam, springs and fabric that hold water. Most of the water goes deep and does not show on the surface. Standing a mattress on its edge and blotting the top will not reach the inside.

If the water was clean and the mattress got wet at the surface only, drying with strong airflow and a dehumidifier can work. If the mattress soaked through, if it sat for days or if mold smell appears, plan to replace it. Do not sleep on a mattress that smells musty.

Take photos of the mattress, the label and the wet area before you move it. Your insurer may want to see it or at least the photos.

Sofas, chairs and cushions

Upholstered furniture has foam, batting and wood frames. Cushions can sometimes be removed and dried, but the base holds water in padding and fabric. A wood frame may dry well, while particleboard swells and fails.

Lift furniture off wet carpet and put blocks of foil or wood under the legs to prevent stains and rust. Move pieces to a dry room with airflow. Do not use a heater or a hair dryer on the fabric, which can set stains and shrink fibers.

Drying, step by step

  1. Remove the item from standing water and lift it onto blocks.
  2. Blot, do not rub, and take cushions off.
  3. Set up fans and a dehumidifier in the room.
  4. Check the inside with a moisture meter if you can.
  5. Decide within 24 to 48 hours, the EPA window for preventing mold.

A restoration crew can do more with moisture meters and drying equipment. Contents cleaning is a service some companies offer, and you can ask whether it applies. The carpet page explains what happens to the floor under the furniture.

Dalton, carpet and furniture

Dalton homes often have carpet under furniture, and a wet sofa on wet carpet will stain both. Furniture legs sitting in water can leave rust marks that are hard to remove, so lifting pieces early matters. Many local homes are on slabs, where water spreads wide and shallow and gets under every piece.

If a storm or a pipe break wets a room in Varnell or Rocky Face, the same rule applies. Lift first, then call. A crew can inventory what is wet and what is dry before pieces are moved.

What to tell your insurer

The Georgia insurance commissioner's office advises listing each damaged item with quantity, description, purchase date and replacement cost. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has examined them. For mattresses and sofas, photograph the label and the model.

If a piece is discarded for safety, such as after contaminated water, photograph it first and note why. Coverage for contents depends on your policy, and the extraction page explains how the first stage of the response works.

Sort belongings into three piles. The first is what is clean and dry, the second is what can be dried and cleaned and the third is what goes. Photograph each pile and keep a list for your insurer.

Photos and papers that matter, such as documents and family pictures, can often be saved if you act fast. Separate wet pages, blot them gently and let them air dry on a clean surface. Specialists exist for valuable items.

Signs it is time to throw it out

  • A musty smell that stays after drying.
  • Visible mold on fabric or foam.
  • The item sat in flood or sewage water.
  • Foam stays damp inside after several days.
  • The frame is swollen or the glue has failed.

What to do first

  • Find and stop the moisture source before anything else.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or fan to chase wet or moldy spots, because that spreads material through the home.
  • Take dated photos of anything affected and keep them with your records.

For drying timing, the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. For claims, the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner publishes plain-language consumer guidelines.

Questions readers ask

Can a wet mattress be saved?

Sometimes, if the water was clean and it was dried quickly. A soaked-through mattress or one that smells musty should be replaced.

How long do I have to dry a sofa?

The EPA recommends drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Is a sofa soaked by flood water salvageable?

Usually not, because contaminated water gets deep into foam and fabric.

Should I use a hair dryer?

No. Heat can set stains and shrink fabric. Use fans, airflow and a dehumidifier.

Will my insurance replace furniture?

If the loss is covered, contents are often covered too, subject to limits. Photograph and list each item.

What if the piece smells musty?

That points to hidden moisture or mold. If drying does not remove the smell, replacement is the safer path.
