How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Dalton, GA

By the Dalton Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Finding the main valve before a leak starts is the cheapest insurance there is. Here is how to find it, test it and use it.

  • 🛡Licensed and insured
  • 📋Insurance documentation help
  • ⏱24/7 emergency line
  • 📍Dalton and nearby towns

Knowing how to shut off your home water main in Dalton can turn a flood into a small mess. Most houses have two places to stop the water: a valve where the line enters the building and a shutoff at the meter. Neither is hard to use, but both are hard to find in a hurry.

This guide covers where to look, how to turn each kind of valve, how to test it and what to do if it is stuck. Take ten minutes this week to locate yours, so you are not searching with a hose of water at your feet.

Where the main valve usually is

The main shutoff is where the water line comes into the house from the street. In houses with a slab, look in a utility closet, a garage wall, the laundry room or near the water heater. In houses with a crawl space or basement, look along the front foundation wall or near the point where a pipe comes out of the ground.

Houses in this region often have the meter in a box near the street or the property line. The utility owns the meter and the valve beside it, and the homeowner owns the line from there to the house. A second shutoff on the house side lets you control water without a meter key.

Two kinds of valves

A gate valve has a round wheel handle. Turn it clockwise, which is to the right, until it stops. These valves can stick or break if forced, so turn gently and do not over-tighten.

A ball valve has a lever handle. Turn it a quarter turn so the lever sits crosswise to the pipe. In the off position the lever is perpendicular to the pipe, and parallel when the water is on.

If the valve is stiff, work it back and forth a few times in a dry moment. A little exercise now keeps it usable later. If it leaks around the stem or will not turn, call a plumber.

Testing that it works

  1. Close the valve all the way.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house and let the line drain.
  3. If water keeps running for more than a minute, the valve is not sealing.
  4. Open the valve again and check for leaks around the stem.
  5. Label the valve with a tag so anyone in the house can find it.

At the street: the meter shutoff

The meter in your yard has a shutoff beside it. Utilities usually want customers to use the valve on the house side, and the utility valve is meant for utility staff. If you cannot reach your own valve, call the utility. Dalton Utilities serves the city and parts of nearby counties, and customers can contact them to ask about shutting off water at the meter.

Use the meter box only if you know what you are doing. A dropped lid, a snake or wasps in the box are real hazards, and the valve may need a special key. Never reach into a box you cannot see.

Knowing which side of the meter is yours helps in a leak. If the leak is between the meter and the house, you pay for the repair. If it is on the street side, the utility handles it.

Fixture shutoffs: stop one thing, not the house

  • Toilets have a small valve behind the base. Turn it clockwise.
  • Sinks have two valves under the cabinet, hot and cold.
  • Washing machines have two valves on the wall.
  • Water heaters have a cold supply valve on top of the tank.
  • Dishwashers and refrigerators have their own valves, often under the sink or behind the unit.

These small valves can stop a leak without shutting down the whole house. They also fail over time, so turn each one once a year. See water heater leaks and appliance leaks for what to do if one fails.

What to do after the water is off

Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, and flush a toilet. Turn off the water heater to keep the tank from heating with no water. Switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Then call for help with the water. A crew extracts and dries, and a plumber repairs the line. If the leak came from a broken pipe, the burst pipe page explains the next steps. Houses in Tunnel Hill and Cohutta with a well need to switch off the pump at the breaker as well.

Common problems with shutoff valves

  • Valves that have not been turned in years can seize or leak at the stem.
  • Handles can break if forced.
  • Some houses have a shutoff that closes only part of the flow.
  • Mobile homes and older houses may have different arrangements.

If you find a valve that does not work, a plumber can replace it, often in under an hour. Do it now, not in the middle of a flood.

A five-minute home check

Walk the house with a phone and take a photo of every shutoff you find. Put the photos in a folder called water. Tell everyone who lives in the home where the main valve is, including teenagers and visitors who stay a while.

Add the number for your plumber and the utility to the same folder. When a pipe splits at midnight, the folder saves you from searching the web with wet hands.

What to do first

  • Find and stop the moisture source before anything else.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or fan to chase wet or moldy spots, because that spreads material through the home.
  • Take dated photos of anything affected and keep them with your records.

For drying timing, the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. For claims, the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner publishes plain-language consumer guidelines.

Questions readers ask

Where is the main water shutoff in most Dalton homes?

Usually where the line enters the house, such as a garage wall, a utility closet or the front foundation wall. A meter box near the street also has a shutoff.

Which way do I turn the valve?

A gate valve turns clockwise to close. A ball valve turns a quarter turn so the lever sits crosswise to the pipe.

What if the valve is stuck?

Do not force it. Try the shutoff at the meter or call the utility, and call a plumber to replace the valve.

Should I turn the water heater off too?

Yes, if the main is closed for more than a short time. A heater with no water can damage the elements or the tank.

Can I shut off just one fixture?

Yes. Most fixtures have a small valve of their own, and using it keeps the rest of the house running.

Who pays for a leak between the meter and the house?

Usually the homeowner. A leak on the street side of the meter is normally the utility's responsibility. Ask your utility to confirm.
