Mold on Walls in Dalton, GA: What Causes It and What to Do

By the Dalton Water Damage Pros team. Published November 10, 2026.

Mold on a wall is a symptom. The cause is moisture in the wall, on the wall or behind it.

  • 📋Insurance documentation help

Mold on walls in Dalton usually points to one of four things. A leak inside the wall, condensation on a cold surface, high indoor humidity or water that came through from outside. Cleaning the spots without finding the cause only buys a few weeks.

This guide walks through the common causes, how to tell what you are looking at and when a wall needs more than a wipe. It follows EPA guidance, and it assumes you want to fix the cause and not just the stain.

Four common reasons walls grow mold

Cause and clue
CauseClueLikely fix
A leak in the wall.Stain with a ring, soft drywall, musty smell.Repair the leak, dry and replace damaged drywall.
Condensation.Growth on cold exterior walls or in corners.Raise airflow, insulate and lower humidity.
High indoor humidity.Growth in bathrooms, closets and basements.Run fans and dehumidifiers.
Water from outside.Staining after rain, bubbling paint.Fix flashing, gutters or grading.

Each of these needs a different fix. A bathroom wall with surface spotting needs better ventilation, while a bedroom wall that stains after rain needs a roofer or a gutter repair.

Mold or dirt?

The EPA does not offer a quick test for telling mold from dirt, and it notes that molds come in many colors. Dirt usually wipes off and stays gone. Mold often returns within days, has a musty smell and may feel fuzzy or slimy.

A simple check is to see whether the spot is on a damp or cold area. If it keeps coming back in the same place, assume moisture. If you are not sure, a technician with a moisture meter can tell you whether the wall is wet.

Why Dalton walls are at risk

Dalton is humid through much of the year, and summer indoor air conditioners can create cold surfaces where moisture condenses. Exterior walls behind furniture, closet corners and rooms over crawl spaces are common spots.

Moisture can linger wherever air does not move, in older districts such as McCarty Subdivision and Thornton Avenue-Murray Hill and in newer homes. A closet against a cold exterior wall can stay damp for weeks. Newer homes have tighter envelopes and can trap moisture if ventilation is poor.

If a leak is the cause, the wall and drywall page explains what drying and repair look like. Homes in Rocky Face sit below the ridge, where storm runoff can reach exterior walls.

What to do first

  1. Check for active leaks under sinks, around windows and on the roof.
  2. Look at the ceiling and the attic above for stains.
  3. Measure indoor humidity. The EPA suggests staying under 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50 percent.
  4. Move furniture away from exterior walls so air can circulate.
  5. If the growth is bigger than about 10 square feet, plan for professional help.

Cleaning small patches

For a small area of surface mold on a hard, non-porous surface, the EPA says you can usually handle it yourself. Wear gloves and eye protection, and use detergent and water. Dry the surface thoroughly afterward.

Porous materials such as drywall, ceiling tile and insulation that are moldy generally need to be removed and replaced. Painting over them does not solve the problem, and the growth continues underneath.

When the wall has to come out

  • The drywall is soft, crumbling or sagging.
  • The growth reaches the paper and the wall stays damp.
  • A moisture meter shows wetness inside the wall.
  • The smell stays after cleaning.
  • The wall sits under a roof or plumbing leak that ran for days.

A remediation crew contains the area, removes the damaged drywall and dries the framing before rebuilding. The mold remediation page describes the process in detail.

Room by room: where wall mold starts

Common rooms and fixes
RoomUsual causeWhat helps
Bathroom.Steam with weak ventilation.Run the fan 20 minutes after a shower and vent outside.
Bedroom exterior wall.Condensation behind furniture.Pull furniture off the wall and lower humidity.
Closet.Still air and a cold exterior wall.Leave the door ajar and add airflow.
Kitchen under the sink.A slow supply or drain leak.Fix the leak and dry the cabinet base.
Basement wall.Seepage or high humidity.Dehumidify and improve exterior drainage.
Laundry room.Hoses and humid dryer air.Check hoses and vent the dryer outside.

Most of these start small. Catching a patch early means a wipe and a fix, while a patch that grows for months means opened walls. Look behind furniture twice a year, and sniff for a musty smell in closed rooms.

A hand-held humidity meter costs very little and gives you a real number. If a room reads above 60 percent for days, the EPA says that is the time to act, whether or not you see growth. The kitchen page covers the cabinet-base leak in detail.

Why paint and sealers do not solve it

Stain-blocking primers can hide a dry stain, and they are a fine finish step once the wall is dry. They do not stop growth in a wall that is still wet. If the surface feels cool or damp or the paint keeps blistering, the moisture is still there.

The order matters. Fix the source, dry the wall, remove what cannot be dried and only then prime and paint. A contractor who offers to paint first is working in the wrong order.

Prevention that works in this climate

Run bath fans during and after showers and vent them outside. Keep a dehumidifier running in damp rooms in summer. Clean gutters and extend downspouts, and slope soil away from the foundation, as the EPA recommends.

If a wall has had a plumbing leak, ask for a moisture reading before painting. A damp wall that is closed up becomes a mold project later. The ceiling repair page covers the same check for ceilings.

What to do first

  • Find and stop the moisture source before anything else.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or fan to chase wet or moldy spots, because that spreads material through the home.
  • Take dated photos of anything affected and keep them with your records.

For drying timing, the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. For claims, the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner publishes plain-language consumer guidelines.

Questions readers ask

Why does mold keep coming back on my wall?

Because the moisture source is still there. Cleaning removes the growth, not the cause.

Is mold on an exterior wall always a leak?

No. Condensation and poor airflow can cause it. A moisture meter shows whether the wall is wet inside.

Can I paint over mold?

No. Painting hides it while growth continues. Remove the growth and fix the moisture first.

Does mold behind furniture matter?

Yes. Pulling furniture a few inches from exterior walls helps air move and prevents damp pockets.

How do Dalton summers affect indoor mold?

The humidity is high and air conditioning creates cold surfaces, so condensation is a common cause. Keep humidity under 60 percent.

When should I call a professional?

If growth is larger than about 10 square feet, if drywall is soft or if the smell and spots return after cleaning.
