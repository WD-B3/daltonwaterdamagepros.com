Commercial water emergency: first steps

  1. Find and close the water source, or call the facility manager to do it.
  2. Protect people first and keep them out of wet areas with live equipment.
  3. Move inventory, files and electronics up and away from the water.
  4. Take photos and video of the loss before anything is moved.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch about size, source and business hours.

Same-day response is offered when available, and the crew can work nights and weekends so the business can reopen sooner. Dispatch will ask whether the building is occupied and whether utilities are on.

Planning around operations

A restoration plan for a business starts with what can stay open. Work can be phased, with the wet area contained and the rest of the building in use. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed so aisles and exits stay clear.

Tenants and landlords have different responsibilities. A lease may say who handles building water damage and who handles contents. Ask for the lease language early, and let the crew document the loss for both parties.

Large floors and big equipment

Commercial slabs and open floors cover a lot of area. Truck-mounted extraction or large pumps move water quickly, and commercial dehumidifiers dry large spaces. For concrete floors, drying can take longer than the extraction.

Warehouses and plants often have storage racks, machinery and pallets. Moving and protecting items is part of the plan, and a crew can raise and block items to keep them out of the water. The structural drying page explains the equipment in detail.

Documentation for insurance and tenants

Business interruption and property claims need records. Photos, moisture logs, equipment lists and daily notes support the claim. The crew keeps these as the job runs and can provide copies for your insurer, your landlord and your accountant.

Georgia guidance says to report claims promptly and take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. For a business, that includes keeping receipts for emergency work, temporary protection and any relocated stock.

Insurance and your policy

Commercial property policies differ widely, and business interruption coverage is often separate. Check your policy for water damage, sewer backup and flood wording, and ask your agent which coverage applies.

Report the claim promptly, document the loss and keep damaged property until the adjuster has seen it. Tenants should also notify the landlord or property manager.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions Dalton homeowners ask

Do you handle commercial water emergencies at night or on weekends in Dalton?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Close the water source if you can and call.

Can the business stay open during drying?

Often a contained area can be dried while the rest operates. The crew will discuss phasing after seeing the loss.

Who pays, the landlord or the tenant?

It depends on the lease and the policies in place. Document the loss and notify both parties.

Do you help with inventory?

The crew can move and protect items during the job. Packing out large stock is scoped separately.

How do I support a business interruption claim?

Keep photos, logs, receipts and records of lost operating time, and give them to your insurer.

What if the water came from a unit next door?

Document the damage, notify the property manager and call. Drying can start while responsibility is sorted out.
