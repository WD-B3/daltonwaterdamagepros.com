Water damage emergencies in Tunnel Hill

Emergency calls from Tunnel Hill look like those from any small Georgia city. Supply lines split, water heaters fail and roofs leak. What changes is the building age, since this is a community with homes and structures that date back generations, and older plumbing and roofs are more likely to fail.

The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day. Same-day response is offered when available, and after-hours work is part of the service. Tell dispatch whether you are on the Whitfield or Catoosa side so the crew can plan the route.

A town built on a railroad tunnel

The Western and Atlantic Railroad tunnel through Chetoogeta Mountain gave the town its name. The brick-lined bore is preserved as a walking trail, and a CSX tunnel runs beside it. The town sits on the west base of the mountain, on a ridge the USGS lists as Tunnel Hill Ridge.

That ridge carries the Tennessee Valley Divide. Rain that falls on one side drains toward the Tennessee River, and rain on the other drains toward the Conasauga River. In practice that means runoff moves quickly off the crest, and low spots at the base of the slope take the water.

If water is still spreading in Tunnel Hill

Shut off the water

Close the main valve. A cutoff is usually where the line enters the house or at the meter.

Cut power where wet

Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry. Otherwise call 911 or your power provider.

Protect what is dry

Lift items from the floor, roll back rugs and move furniture away from the wet area.

Document

Take photos and a short video of the water and the damage.

Older buildings and water

Tunnel Hill has homes from several eras, and some structures date to the Civil War period. The Clisby Austin House, which served as General Sherman's headquarters while he planned attacks on Dalton and Resaca, is one of the best known. Older frame buildings can have original wood floors, plaster walls and older pipe.

Plaster and old wood dry differently from modern drywall and laminate. A crew uses meters to decide what can be dried in place. The hardwood floor page and the wall page explain how.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.
