Storm damage emergency: what to do now

Make the area safe first. Stay clear of downed power lines, hanging limbs and any ceiling that sags with water. If a tree is on the house, leave the structure if it feels unstable and call 911 for anything involving fire, gas or injury.

Then protect what you can. Move furniture and electronics away from leaks, catch drips and photograph everything before cleanup. Call the 24/7 line. Same-day response is offered when available, though after a widespread storm the line can be busy, and dispatch will tell you where you are in the queue.

Temporary protection and the claim

Most policies require you to protect the property from further damage. Tarps on the roof, boards over windows and plastic over openings are normal. Keep the receipts, and photograph the damage before and after the temporary work.

A roofer or general contractor handles permanent repairs. We handle the water, drying and documentation. Georgia guidance says to report promptly, keep damaged property for the adjuster and make reasonable repairs.

How the cleanup works

  1. Standing water is extracted and wet contents are moved to a dry area.
  2. Moisture mapping finds wet insulation, ceilings and wall cavities.
  3. Wet insulation and saturated drywall are removed, and the framing is opened to air.
  4. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the structure until readings are normal.
  5. Photos and logs support your insurance file.

Rainwater is not as clean as it seems. It runs over roofing, gutters and soil before it reaches your ceiling, so crews treat it with care. The drying page explains how hidden moisture is handled.

Insurance and your policy

Wind and rain damage from a storm is often covered under homeowners policies, though flood is usually excluded. Some policies have separate wind or hail deductibles. Read your policy and ask your insurer which applies.

Georgia guidance says to report promptly and make reasonable repairs to protect the property. Keep the receipts and the damaged materials for the adjuster.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions Dalton homeowners ask

Do you handle storm water emergencies at night or on weekends in Dalton?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. After a widespread storm the line may be busy, and dispatch will tell you what to expect.

Is storm water damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Often, when rain enters through a storm-damaged opening. Flood is usually excluded. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Should I tarp the roof myself?

Roofs are dangerous in wet weather. A roofer can place a tarp safely.

Do I need to dry the attic?

Yes, if insulation or framing got wet. Wet attic insulation holds moisture against the ceiling below.

How long until mold can grow?

The EPA recommends drying within 24 to 48 hours to prevent growth.

Who repairs the roof?

A roofer does. We dry the structure and document the loss.
