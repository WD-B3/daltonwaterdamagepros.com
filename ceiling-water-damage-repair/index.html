A stained, sagging or dripping ceiling means water is above it. Finding the source comes first, and repair follows once everything is dry.

Emergency ceiling water damage repair in Dalton, GA covers a ceiling that is dripping, sagging, bubbling or showing a spreading brown stain. The water above it can come from a roof, a bathroom, a supply line or an air handler, and each source needs a different fix.

If water is coming through right now, put a bucket under the drip and move furniture out of the way. If the ceiling is bulging, do not stand beneath it, because trapped water can bring down drywall without warning. Poke a small hole at the lowest point of the bulge with a screwdriver to let water drain into a bucket, then call the 24/7 line.

When a leaking ceiling is an emergency

A ceiling leak is an emergency when water is still coming through, the drywall is bulging or the leak is near a light fixture. Water on a light fixture or wiring is an electrical hazard, so turn off the circuit at the panel if it is dry and safe to reach, and stay away from anything that looks wet near wiring.

Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch where the water is. Same-day response is offered when available, and after-hours work is part of the service. While you wait, move furniture, cover what you cannot move with plastic and keep the area clear.

A single drip can still be a large loss. Insulation above the drywall can hold gallons of water, and the ceiling can fail without much warning.

Roof leaks and ceilings: one job

The ceiling is where roof leaks show up inside the house, so this page covers both. Water from a failed shingle, a cracked pipe boot or poor flashing travels down through the roof decking and insulation before it stains the ceiling, and the stain is often several feet from the real leak.

A roofer fixes the roof. We dry what got wet below it. That includes insulation, the ceiling drywall and any framing, and we document it for your insurer. The two trades work together, and the roof needs to be sealed first or the drying does not last.

Roof leak repair is a roofing question, not a restoration question. If you need a roofer, ask for a written scope and take photos of the roof damage before and after. Georgia insurance guidance says to make reasonable repairs to protect the house, and a temporary tarp is a common step.

Finding the source above the stain

  • Check the attic above the stain for wet insulation, dark wood or a drip trail along rafters.
  • Look at bathrooms and laundry rooms upstairs for failed seals, supply connections or overflow marks.
  • Inspect the HVAC drain pan and condensate line if the air handler is in the attic.
  • Look at the roof after rain for missing shingles, lifted flashing or damaged vent boots.

If the stain shows up only after rain, the roof is the first suspect. If it appears on a dry day, a supply or drain line is more likely. A moisture meter and a thermal camera help narrow the search without opening the whole ceiling.

How the ceiling is dried and repaired

  1. Water is stopped and drained from any bulge, and wet insulation is removed.
  2. The ceiling drywall is checked with a meter. Saturated or sagging sections are cut out.
  3. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the framing and the surrounding cavity.
  4. Drywall is replaced, textured and painted once readings are normal.
  5. Stains on drywall that is dry can be sealed with a stain-blocking primer before paint.

A stain alone is not a reason to replace a ceiling, and a dry stain that stays the same size is cosmetic. A ceiling that is soft, sagging or still wet needs to come down. When walls are also wet, the wall and drywall page explains how that side is handled.

Insurance and your policy

Sudden water from a plumbing failure is often covered. Roof leaks are often covered when caused by a storm and not by wear or lack of maintenance, and policies differ. Gradual leaks and neglect are frequently excluded, so read your policy and ask your insurer which applies.

Photograph the stain, the attic and the roof if it is safe. Keep any damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them, and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions Dalton homeowners ask

Do you handle ceiling leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Dalton?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. If a ceiling is bulging or water is near a light fixture, call right away.

Can I just paint over a water stain on my ceiling?

Only if the area is dry and the leak is fixed. Painting over a wet ceiling hides the problem and can trap moisture. Use a meter or have the area checked first.

Should I cut a hole in a bulging ceiling?

A small hole at the lowest point of a bulge, with a bucket below, can drain trapped water and prevent a larger collapse. Stand to the side and keep clear of wiring.

How do I know if the roof or a pipe caused the leak?

If the stain grows after rain, suspect the roof. If it grows on dry days, suspect plumbing or an HVAC drain line. A professional can confirm with a meter and a camera.

Does the insulation need to be replaced?

Wet insulation is usually removed because it holds moisture against the framing. Dry insulation can often stay.

Who fixes the roof?

A roofer does. We dry the damage below it and document the loss. Keep the roofer's estimate with your claim file.
