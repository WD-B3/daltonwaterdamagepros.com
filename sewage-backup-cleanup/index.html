Emergency sewage backup cleanup in Dalton, GA deals with wastewater that came up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or cleanout. If sewage is on your floor, keep people and pets out of the area, avoid touching the water and call the 24/7 line.

Do not flush toilets or run water, because anything you send down may come back up. Stay away from the affected room with bare feet or open cuts, and keep children out. If the backup is a plumbing blockage in your own line, a plumber clears it, and we clean up the contamination.

Sewage backup emergency: what to do first

  • Keep everyone out of the wet area, including pets.
  • Do not use toilets, sinks, showers or the washer, since they all feed the same drain.
  • Turn off power to the area only if you can reach the panel without stepping into the water.
  • Open windows for air if the weather allows, but do not use fans to spread contaminated air.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe how far the water reached.

Same-day response is offered when available, and after-hours work is part of the service. Sewage should be removed quickly, because contaminated moisture soaks deeper into porous materials the longer it sits.

Why a backup happens

Backups come from a clog in your own lateral line, a blockage in the municipal main or a failed pump in a home with a lift station. Tree roots, grease, wipes and collapsed older pipe are common causes. A backup that shows up in several fixtures at once, such as a floor drain and a toilet together, points to a main-line problem.

If neighbors have the same problem, the blockage may be in the public main. Call the utility that serves your address. Dalton Utilities handles the sewer system for the city and parts of the surrounding area, and a plumber can tell you whether the clog is on your side of the cleanout.

Contaminated water and health

Sewage can carry bacteria, viruses and parasites. Skin contact and splashing are the main risks, and children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems are more vulnerable. If anyone has contact and develops symptoms, they should see a physician.

The industry standard for water damage, ANSI/IICRC S500, classifies this as Category 3 water and calls for protective equipment and removal of affected porous materials. A company that offers to dry carpet in place after sewage is cutting a corner that matters for your family.

How sewage cleanup works

  1. The crew sets up protective barriers and wears protective equipment.
  2. Liquid is extracted and solid waste is removed and disposed of properly.
  3. Carpet, pad, baseboards, insulation and drywall touched by the water are removed and bagged.
  4. Surfaces that stay, such as concrete and framing, are cleaned and disinfected.
  5. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the space, and a final check confirms readings are normal.

After cleanup, odor and air quality matter. Ask the crew about air scrubbers or filtration if odors linger. If damp materials stay in place, the mold remediation page explains what to look for.

Insurance and your policy

Many homeowners policies exclude sewer backup unless an endorsement was added. Some policies offer water backup coverage with a set limit. Check your policy for sewer or drain backup wording and ask your insurer.

Report the claim promptly and photograph the damage before cleanup. Keep records of emergency work, as Georgia guidance advises.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions Dalton homeowners ask

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Dalton?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Keep everyone out of the area and call.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

Small spills on a hard floor can be handled with protective gear and disinfectant, but a backup into carpet or drywall needs professional removal.

Does homeowners insurance cover sewer backup?

Often not without an endorsement. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Is the carpet always thrown out after sewage?

Usually yes, along with the pad. Category 3 water makes porous materials unsafe to keep.

Who fixes the clog?

A plumber clears it. If the blockage is in the public main, the utility handles it.

How long until the house is safe again?

After removal, disinfection and drying, a final check confirms the area. The time depends on the extent of the loss.
