Water damage emergencies in Varnell

Varnell grew quickly after it became a city in 1968, and its population rose from 358 in 1990 to 1,491 in 2000 and 2,179 in 2020. That growth means many houses here are newer than those in the older parts of Dalton. Newer homes have their own failure points, such as supply lines, water heaters and appliance hoses.

The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Same-day response is offered when available. If you have children at home, say so when you call, so the crew can plan how to protect bedrooms and play areas.

Coahulla Creek and local flooding

Coahulla Creek runs near Dalton, and the National Weather Service gauge near the Keiths Mill Road bridge uses a flood stage of 16 feet. At that level, minor flooding covers pastures and fields on both banks upstream of the bridge. The creek rose above flood stage during the September 2009 floods, which also affected Whitfield County more broadly.

In 2025, a storm dropped 6.71 inches of rain on the Greater Dalton area in roughly 30 to 45 minutes, according to the National Weather Service, and Whitfield County fire crews evacuated five homes. Fast downpours like that overwhelm drains even away from a creek.

For water that comes from outside, the flood damage page explains the safety steps and the cleanup. The basement page covers lower levels.

Many Varnell houses are slab or crawl space homes with drywall interiors. Hidden leaks in walls or under slabs can run for weeks, and a rising water bill is a common clue. The slab leak page explains how those are found.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Does homeowners insurance cover creek flooding?

Usually not. Flood is commonly excluded and needs a separate policy. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Can a crew work around a family with children?

Yes. Tell dispatch who is in the home. The crew can set up so that bedrooms and play areas are protected.

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

What should I photograph?
