What the city has said about water in Brookwood

The City of Dalton has described Tar Creek as a creek that repeatedly flooded Brookwood Park and nearby homes. To reduce that, it bought and demolished the house at 915 Brookwood Drive and graded the lot into a flood storage area. The lot slows runoff from Stoneleigh Drive and Lakemont before the water reaches the creek.

That is the documented history, and it applies to the area around the creek. It does not mean every street in Brookwood has flooded. Many calls in any neighborhood come from inside the house, such as a split supply line, a failed water heater or a dishwasher hose.

Water from a creek is not the same as water from a pipe

Water that comes up from a creek or runs in from the yard can carry soil and bacteria. Crews treat it as contaminated, which changes the work. Soaked carpet and padding usually come out, and hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Water from a broken supply line is cleaner at the start, but it does not stay that way for long if it sits. Either way, the next step is the same. Get the water out, find where it traveled and dry the structure with equipment that is measured and checked. The flood damage page explains the contaminated case in detail.

Drying a single-level brick ranch

On one level, water that starts in a kitchen or utility room can move into hallways and bedrooms along the floor. The crew checks the rooms around the wet area with moisture meters, because water often travels farther than it looks. Baseboards, the lower part of walls and the floor system are the usual places to test.

Extraction comes first with water extraction equipment. Drying follows with air movers and dehumidifiers, which is covered on the structural drying page. Readings are taken again each visit until the wet materials reach their drying goals.

Insurance notes for Brookwood homeowners

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the policy. A sudden pipe failure and rising creek water are often treated differently, so read the policy and ask your insurer before you assume either way. We do not decide coverage and we cannot promise an outcome.

What you can do is document the loss. Photograph the water and the damage before anything is moved, keep wet items when you can, and ask for moisture readings and an itemized scope of work. Those records help when you talk to the insurer.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions from Brookwood homeowners

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Brookwood?

Yes. Dispatch answers around the clock, including nights and weekends. If a pipe or appliance is running, stop it if you safely can, then call with your street.

Does your service area include all of Brookwood?

We have no official Brookwood boundary to quote, so give dispatch your street and it will confirm. Homes on the west side between Walnut Avenue and downtown fall inside the Dalton service area.

Has Brookwood flooded before?

The City of Dalton says Tar Creek repeatedly flooded Brookwood Park and nearby homes, and the city built flood storage on Brookwood Drive to reduce it. That does not mean every address floods.

Is creek water different from a broken pipe?

Yes. Creek and yard water can carry soil and bacteria, so porous materials are often removed and surfaces are cleaned and disinfected. Clean supply water is simpler at first but must be dried fast.

Can I save my carpet after a leak?

Sometimes. It depends on the water source, how long it sat and what the pad is like. The crew tests before deciding and will tell you which materials can stay.

What should I photograph before cleanup?

Take wide shots of each wet room, close shots of damaged items and the water line on walls. Do it only where it is safe, and keep out of rooms where water touches electrical outlets.
