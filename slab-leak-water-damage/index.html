Emergency slab leak water damage in Dalton, GA starts with a supply pipe that leaks under or inside a concrete foundation. If a floor feels warm, water appears at a wall base or your bill jumps with no change in use, close the main valve and call.

Slab leaks are hidden by definition. The pipe is under the floor, and you see the effects instead: damp carpet, lifting laminate, a musty smell, a sound of running water at night or a water meter that moves when everything is off. Those clues tell you to act before the slab leak damages the flooring and walls.

Slab leak emergency: first steps

Close the main water valve to stop the leak, then check the meter. If the dial keeps moving with every fixture off, the leak is on the house side of the meter. A plumber can then use electronic leak detection to locate the pipe under the slab.

Call us for the water that came up into the house. Same-day response is offered when available, and the crew can work after hours. We do not break the slab or repair the pipe, and the plumber does that while we handle the drying.

Why slab leaks happen

Pipes in a slab are exposed to the concrete around them, and the surface of copper can wear from soil chemistry, movement or abrasion at tight bends. Pressure, hot water and age all add stress. Houses in older parts of town can still have pipes of the era, and a plumber can tell you what your house has.

Northwest Georgia clay soils shrink and swell with the weather, and a foundation shifts slightly with them. That movement can strain a pipe that was fine for years, particularly where it enters or leaves the slab.

How the water gets into the house

Water under a slab pushes up through control joints, along the edges and around plumbing penetrations. It travels through wall bottoms by wicking. The first sign is often a damp baseboard or a flooring seam that lifts.

Carpet and pad over a slab can hide the moisture. A meter on the floor surface and a thermal camera show where the water is, and a dehumidification plan is built around that map. See carpet water damage for how wet carpet and pad are handled.

How the drying is done

  1. The plumber locates and repairs the leak, or reroutes the line overhead.
  2. Wet carpet, pad and any swollen flooring are removed as needed.
  3. Baseboards come off and wall bottoms are opened so air can reach the wet cavity.
  4. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the slab surface and the walls, and meters track progress.
  5. Readings and photos are logged for your insurer.

Concrete holds a lot of moisture and dries slowly. That is why the drying portion can take longer than the extraction stage, and why meter readings, not time on the clock, decide when equipment comes out. The structural drying page explains the process.

Insurance and your policy

Policies treat slab leaks differently. Some cover the resulting water damage, and many exclude the cost of tearing out the slab to reach the pipe. Long-term leaks are often excluded. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Keep the plumber's leak detection report, photos and meter logs. Georgia guidance says to report promptly and take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

What to do first, before help arrives

  • Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or a breaker panel, and shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Stop the source if you can, such as the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Move valuables and small furniture out of the wet area, and do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Take dated photos before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

Our timing advice follows the EPA guidelines on mold and moisture, which say wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Claim steps follow the consumer guidance from the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner.

Questions Dalton homeowners ask

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Dalton?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Close the main valve, then call.

How do I confirm a slab leak?

Turn off all water and watch the meter. A moving dial means a leak. A plumber can then locate it with electronic detection.

Do you fix the pipe under the slab?

No. A plumber does that. We dry the building and document the loss.

How long does a slab take to dry?

Concrete dries slowly, and the time depends on how wet it got and the humidity. Meter readings decide when drying is done.

Will the flooring have to be replaced?

Carpet pad often does. Laminate and wood may need replacement if they swelled, and tile may stay if the subfloor dries.

Does insurance cover slab leaks?

Coverage varies by policy. Many cover water damage from a sudden leak but not tearing into the slab. Confirm with your insurer.
